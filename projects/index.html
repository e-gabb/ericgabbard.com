Backflip · Acquisition funnel → loan application

Running the funnel on experiments, not opinions

ProblemFunnel decisions were being made on conviction. Everyone could see drop-off; nobody could say which drop-off actually cost applications. And the one decision big enough to matter — how much rate information the loan application should show, and when — had two senior stakeholders on opposite sides, each with a plausible story about which funnel shape proved them right.

ImpactLed a cross-functional pod through 10+ A/B tests against the real drop-off points instead of shipping a redesign and hoping. Then designed the three-variant application experiment so it could actually settle the argument: a funnel-shape-neutral primary metric, defined before launch, that neither side could reinterpret afterward.

Backflip · Retention & vendor cost

Cutting the right thing

ProblemAnalyzer — the deal-analysis tool investors used to evaluate properties — carried a large third-party data bill and no revenue line of its own. In a cost review it read as an obvious cut. Nobody had put a number on what it was worth.

ImpactInterviewed investors, then ran the retention and conversion analysis that reframed the decision. Traced nearly all the spend to an inbox-scanning feature that surfaced deals automatically — cut it without losing the analysis itself, and renegotiated to usage-based pricing.

  • 85% more applications per user

Backflip · Budgets, draws & disbursements

Rebuilding the system we were renting

ProblemConstruction budgets arrived as spreadsheets over email, and every budget and draw after closing lived on a third-party platform we paid heavily for. It was the ledger, the borrower’s draw experience, and the servicing and accounting review queue — and none of it was ours to fix. Draw speed is one of the reasons a borrower picks a lender, and we couldn’t move it.

ImpactReplaced it in two stages: first budget submission and review inside underwriting, so a deal stopped waiting on a spreadsheet; then the full draw system — borrower request, servicing and accounting review, disbursement. Retired the vendor, cut the payout clock, and a borrower who’d used both told me the new one was the easier system.

  • Vendor retired, ~$275K/yr
  • Draw payout 3.5 → 2.1 days
  • Budgets in-platform
